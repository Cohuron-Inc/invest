The lens the corpus itself supplies

Read together, the six accounts describe one market. The ten-year sits near 4.7% and oil near $90 after tanker strikes in the Strait of Hormuz. The Fed under Warsh held at 3.50 to 3.75% on 29 July with three dissents for a hike, and by 31 August overnight-index swaps priced a 56% chance of a September hike. Payrolls printed +162K against +55K expected on 4 September. Momentum unwound in July at the worst pace since 2009 by Goldman's high-beta index. Chinese models (Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8 Max) pushed stocktalkweekly, an AI bull for three years, to question frontier-lab pricing power. deerpointmacro spent the whole window underweight long-dated hyperscaler bonds because issuance volume, not credit quality, is widening spreads.

Portfolio implication. With 30-year single-A industrial paper yielding 6.02% (deerpointmacro, 5 Sep), the hurdle for equity is a real free-cash-flow yield or a demonstrated earnings inflection. Long-duration stories with commercialization years away are the wrong shape for this rate regime, however good the story. That rule alone moves ASTS, RKLB and ADUR out of core regardless of conviction.

Two accounts are worth noting as counterweights. LogicalThesis argues tech chops through year-end because the market now punishes capex even when the return on it shows up, and wrote on 28 August that AI usage growth may decelerate as heavy users plateau. TheLongInvest expects an index correction from September and says only names that already completed their own correction are safe. Both views push toward the same place: de-rated quality with prints behind it.

How each name was probed

  • Theme. Is the driver structural (cloud consumption, security, GLP-1, memory scarcity) or a flow story?
  • Sentiment. Where is the name on the hated-to-crowded axis, and does more than one account agree?
  • Earnings. Is there an actual print with consensus comparisons in the corpus, or only a chart?
  • Analyst and insider. Any sell-side initiation, target change or Form 4 cluster relayed by an account.
  • Risk. Balance sheet, binary events, concentration, and how reliable the sponsoring account is.

Deep-inside signals no account turned into a pick

CEOStockWatcher reports filings, not stances, so the extraction correctly refused to count its feed as calls. Read as data, the feed holds the best medium-risk leads in the corpus. Each needs the one question the corpus cannot answer: why the stock was down.

  • BSX Boston Scientific. CEO bought $9M on 6 Aug, his first purchase in 4,263 days after 29 straight sales, with three other insiders following. Stock was down 48% in six months. A reversal of that size at a large-cap medtech is the single strongest insider signal in the window. Verify the cause of the drawdown first.
  • FLUT Flutter. Ken Dart, a 10% owner, crossed $2B accumulated, including $160M of swaps on 10 Aug, with the stock down 66% in a year.
  • AMRZ Amrize. Six or more insiders bought the post-earnings, post-spin dip through August and September. CEOStockWatcher frames the weakness as sector-wide construction materials, not company-specific.
  • ELAN Elanco. Five insiders bought the post-earnings dip; a director's largest-ever purchase on 24 Aug.
  • APTV Aptiv. A director's $5M and the CEO's $2.5M, both largest-ever, after a 21% monthly drop.
  • VST Vistra. CEO bought twice in 30 days, the second his largest ever at $903K. TLI reports heavy reader demand for the chart. The AI-power theme with an insider behind it.
  • KLAR Klarna. CEO bought $10M after the earnings drop, the first insider trade in the company's listed life.
  • MITK Mitek. stocktalkweekly's fundamental case: 84% gross margin, 17x FCF, ~20% growth with the SaaS segment at +36%, a top-five US bank displacing its incumbent after a pilot. Small cap, but the numbers are value-shaped.
  • KURA Kura Oncology. The one cross-account corroboration among small caps: LogicalThesis pitched it at 0.80 conviction on a sub-$200M enterprise value for an approved product, and CEOStockWatcher logged the CEO's $1.24M buy plus two other insiders on 25 Aug. Biotech risk keeps it out of core.
  • CSGP CoStar. Founder-CEO's third dip buy at $2.5M with the stock down 69% in a year. Homes.com spending is the unanswered question.
  • ELV · TSCO · CEG · HUBS each show a CEO or multi-insider dip buy in the window, at lower intensity.

Contested names, and how to read the disagreement

MU
Five accounts. StockSavvyShay is long the cycle (UBS models a near-tripling to $379B by FY28; Samsung committed ~70% of memory capacity through 2031; spot HBM3E ~5x contract). TheLongInvest is short on wave structure and by 4 Sep says the bulls must flip $1,000. SK Hynix missed on 29 July, and BofA's July survey had 82% calling long semis the most crowded trade. Read: a trade with excellent fundamentals and crowded positioning. Not core for this mandate.
META
StockSavvyShay relays Bernstein (nearly half of incremental digital ad dollars captured in Q2, could pass Search by year-end) and BofA (agentic distribution to billions). TheLongInvest says FCF is falling and price follows it to the 200 WMA near $488. Both can be right in sequence. Wait for the FCF argument to resolve.
ZETA
TheLongInvest's 2026 top pick (21 consecutive beat-and-raises, FCF +78% YoY). stocktalkweekly calls it a value trap and a retail cult. Small-cap ad-tech with a promotional sponsor; skip for the mandate.
ORCL
Below its 200 WMA in July, Burry short, and StockSavvyShay notes ~6x 2028 estimated profit. LogicalThesis recalls it was punished 60% on OpenAI fears. Debt-funded capex plus frontier-lab counterparty exposure is exactly the risk stocktalkweekly flagged in Nvidia's vendor financing. Cheap for a reason; deep-value satellite at most.
NVDA
Baird's $500 target and JPMorgan's "could more than double without supply constraints" sit against stocktalkweekly's red flag that Nvidia is extending credit to frontier labs to buy its own product, and LogicalThesis's observation that QQQ went nowhere after a monster quarter. Hold at existing size. Not an add.
NBIS
Three accounts long; the highest mention-level convergence in the corpus. Contracted book ~$5.9B across Microsoft and Meta. stocktalkweekly, who holds it, says neoclouds are the hardest part of the buildout to value because GPU depreciation and rental pricing are unknowable. Satellite only.

Satellites: the accounts' loudest calls

These have real evidence but a risk the mandate cannot carry at core size. Cap the sleeve.

  • ASTS and RKLB both got Berenberg Buy initiations on 2 Sep (targets $92 and $83). Two accounts hold ASTS at 0.91 average conviction. Rocket Lab's backlog is up 137% to $2.36B with Iridium adding ~$900M of recurring revenue. Still long-duration in a 4.7% ten-year world.
  • CRDO beat and guided above (Q1 revenue $479M vs $472M, Q2 guide $530M vs $517M) but operating income missed and two customers are over half of revenue. StockSavvyShay's top growth add.
  • WGS · STLN · XGN are LogicalThesis's earnings-gap winners with fundamentals he actually argued (WGS at 4x sales, 70% gross margin, 30% growth; STLN capitated revenue doubling to $300M at 30% margin). He runs margin and stops fast, and he owned a loss on QTRX the same month. Small-cap healthcare sleeve, not core.
  • HIMS raised FY26 revenue guidance to $3.1 to 3.3B and adjusted EBITDA to $275 to 325M; BofA nudged its target to $37 in July. OSCR grew revenue 70% and members 30% with Q2 FCF of $2.1B versus $0.5B a year earlier. Both are high-beta and TLI-sponsored.
  • BABA (cloud +45 to 53%, capex +75%, 62% below its 2020 high) and SE (Q2 revenue +48%, gross profit +47%) have hard numbers behind TLI's targets. Geopolitics sets the size.
  • TSLA beat on revenue, missed on EPS and gross margin. Two accounts long on robotaxi and robotics. Story stock.
  • ADUR has TLI's highest conviction and no earnings evidence anywhere in the corpus. Pass. NKE and LULU are turnaround bets where even TLI concedes the cost is time; LULU fell 18% on its print on 4 Sep.

What this probe cannot tell you yet

The corpus has no price layer, so nothing here is a return, and none of the account performance claims were checked. Every figure is what an account posted. StockSavvyShay covers ten days, not two months. Before acting on the insider names, confirm the cause of each drawdown from filings; the feed reports the buy, never the reason for the dip.

Three next steps would make the next probe sharper: run the price enrichment so 200 WMA and post-earnings claims can be scored; promote CEOStockWatcher's filings to a first-class insider layer rather than treating them as posts; and re-run the account extraction for StockSavvyShay once the timeline endpoint paginates past 29 August.
