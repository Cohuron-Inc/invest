How the signals were read

  • Fundamentals. The last print against consensus and whether the guide was raised, held or cut; the direction of estimate revisions over 90 days; cash conversion and stock compensation; the twelve-month case as the company states it; share and gross-margin trend against the two nearest competitors.
  • Flows and positioning. Open-market Form 4 buys and sells in 90 days, exercises and tax sales excluded; the latest 13F cycle's holder counts and named funds, dated 30 June so ten weeks stale; short interest; and how crowded the name is among the six corpus accounts, read as a contrarian signal.
  • Price and chart. Close against the average, high and low sell-side targets; reward to the average against risk to the nearest anchor; position against the 50-day and 200-day lines, relative strength against the sector, and whether the last print gap held.
  • Context. A rate regime that has flipped from pricing cuts to pricing roughly 1.5 hikes by December, with the ten-year at 4.77% and the real ten-year at 2.42%; WTI up 11.6% in a month on the Iran escalation; value and high beta leading, momentum and quality lagging; and whether each name's duration and sector fit that.
Reading rule. Upside alone is not runway. A 60% gap to consensus with insiders selling, a guide cut and the chart below its 200-day line is a target the Street has not yet lowered. Four gates decide the tier: upside, institutional flow, insider activity and delivery. The score decides the order inside it. And the tier decides nothing about size: the gates are blind to cash burn, which is exactly what the top of this page shows.

Fit to the mandate

No name in the Runway tier fits the mandate. IONQ is the only name passing all four gates and it fails every risk test at once: 53.3% below its high, 11.18% short interest, top-decile volatility, and a thesis that needs the rate regime to change while the market prices roughly 1.5 hikes by December. Its edge is real but thin, the doctrine's estimates-up-price-down setup, and its expected return of +1.6% does not clear a 5.37% hurdle. If it is held at all it is a capped sleeve with the kill date written down, never core.

Against the risk screen alone, one name passes outright. PLPC is 21.1% off its high with 5.03% short interest, no binary event in three months and a neutral macro fit, and its edge is that the market has not repriced a business whose margins are visibly widening. It cannot be sized on this probe's arithmetic because no live target exists to measure against; it can be sized on the print, and that is a judgment the probe is telling you it cannot make for you.

Two names are the right shape and the wrong price, and both are waiting on a number rather than on news. TPL carries an expected return of +6.0% against the 5.37% hurdle, which is sixty basis points for taking oil beta, a 25.5% bear case and a two-analyst consensus spanning $245 to $639. That is not compensation. It becomes interesting nearer $269. QCOM carries +4.1%, below the hurdle, with the cheapest multiple in its peer set at 16.6x and $9,979M of free cash flow; its edge is timing and it becomes a buy nearer $150. Hold what you own in both; add on price, not on this page.

GLD is sized as a macro hedge rather than scored as an equity. The case for it is genuinely two-sided right now: central banks buying roughly 60 tonnes a month, a dollar down 1.55% and an accelerating oil and geopolitics shock on one side, against a 2.42% real ten-year and a live September hike on the other. The hawkish side is the one currently moving the price, which fell 1.3% on the payrolls beat. The FOMC on 16 September is the event, and it is the only company-confirmed date in this entire probe.

The rest of the sleeve is capped or empty. RKT, TEM and AMKR each fail two or more risk tests and none carries an expected return above the hurdle. The honest summary of eight names is that none of them is a core buy at these prices, which is a useful answer rather than an evasive one: three are waiting on a price, one is waiting on coverage, and four are waiting on the business.

What this probe cannot tell you

The 13F layer is dated 30 June and reached you ten weeks later, and on six of the eight names it is not a true quarter-over-quarter holders table at all but MarketBeat's trailing-twelve-month buyer and seller counts, used as the closest available proxy. The CalSTRS row with multi-thousand-percent increases was excluded on every affected name; on TPL its share count exceeded total shares outstanding, which confirms the corruption. Yahoo's estimate-revision tables failed on five of eight names and StockAnalysis publishes no revision table at all, so the 90-day direction for IONQ and TEM was derived from the pre-print consensus against the current consensus and is labelled derived wherever it appears. Seven of the eight next-earnings dates are aggregator estimates, not company-confirmed; the only confirmed date in the probe is the FOMC on 16 September. Chart reads come from aggregator moving-average distances, not a full price series. Prospect, competition, macro fit and narrative harmony are analyst judgments and are written as such. Targets were not checked against the broker notes themselves.

Four things the rubric got wrong on this run, all recorded in the registry so the next probe does not rediscover them. Reward to risk explodes when the downside anchor sits on top of the close: QCOM's 200-day line is 0.54% below its close, giving 26.9:1, and RKT's low target sits six cents below its close, giving 60.7:1. Both are arithmetic, not analysis, and neither was allowed to drive a verdict. The delivery gate cannot be passed by a company that issues no guidance, which is why PLPC fails it after an 86% EPS beat, and why TPL fails it too. Zero corpus coverage scores as "quiet with fundamentals", so five of eight names took 6 to 9 contrarian points for silence and their totals are inflated by about that much. And GLD was scored against equity gates it cannot meet, taking 3 points for having no insiders.

Retail interest is the weakest column here and you were right to expect that. Three of eight names appear in the corpus at all: TEM with 11 posts from 2 accounts, AMKR with 3, QCOM with 2. Only one corpus post is evidence rather than commentary, and it is a good one: CEOStockWatcher's relay on 10 August that an Amkor director sold $2.75M, their second-largest sale out of 53. The two formal stances, stocktalkweekly long AMKR and LogicalThesis long TEM, are both 0.60 conviction and both single-source. Nothing here is crowded, which under the rubric is a positive contrarian read, and for five names it is simply an absent one.

Four verdicts were registered at the 4 September close: IONQ, RKT, TPL and QCOM, each with its scenario tree, expected return, kill criterion and size. The next probe should open with how they did.

Three things would sharpen the next runway probe: a real price series so chart structure and print-gap behaviour are measured rather than inferred from moving-average distances; a fix to the anchor rule so a support level inside 5% of the close is rejected instead of producing a 60:1 ratio; and a separate, shorter rubric for non-operating instruments so a commodity trust is not scored against earnings and insider gates it can never pass.
