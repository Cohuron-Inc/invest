# C3.ai (NYSE: AI) deep dive — 2026-09-18

## Verdict

C3.ai is a speculative turnaround and potential short-squeeze vehicle, not yet a proven high-quality multi-year compounder. The balance sheet gives management time, but revenue is shrinking, gross margins are weak, stock-based compensation and dilution are high, and the current valuation already assumes a successful recovery. Tom Siebel's widely reported 6.17-million-share "purchase" was an option exercise rather than an open-market purchase and should receive less conviction weight.

## What changed

Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue was $52.4 million, down 25.5% year over year. Subscription revenue was $49.2 million, down approximately 18%, and professional services revenue fell approximately 68%. Management called revenue stabilized because it was slightly above Q4's $51.6 million, while bookings rose 73% sequentially and 22 agreements closed.

The recovery is not yet visible in annual guidance. FY2027 revenue guidance of $210 million-$240 million implies a decline of approximately 4%-16% from FY2026 revenue of $250.3 million, or about 10% at the midpoint. Q2 guidance of $51 million-$55 million is roughly flat sequentially at the midpoint.

Founder Thomas Siebel resumed the CEO role in May 2026 after Stephen Ehikian's approximately eight-month tenure. On June 1, he exercised options for 6.17 million shares at an $11.16 exercise price, requiring approximately $68.8 million. The Form 4 used transaction code M, not code P for an open-market purchase. He subsequently transferred the shares to an affiliated investment entity. The cash commitment is mildly positive, but it is materially weaker evidence than a discretionary open-market purchase and increased the share count.

The clearest 2026 open-market purchase was director Jim Snabe's March 9 purchase of 25,000 shares at $9.00, totaling $225,000. Snabe sold 20,000 shares on March 27 at approximately $7.90 and later announced that he would leave the board, leaving only a weak net insider-buy signal. Recent September filings primarily show equity vesting and automatic sales for tax withholding rather than discretionary purchases.

## Earnings quality and dilution

Q1 free cash flow was positive $2.1 million, but GAAP net loss was $92.8 million and stock-based compensation was $59.2 million. SBC equaled approximately 113% of revenue and 64% of the net loss. Positive free cash flow therefore relied heavily on non-cash compensation and working-capital timing rather than operating profitability.

Weighted-average diluted shares rose from 135.4 million to 155.0 million year over year, approximately 14.5%. Period-end shares increased approximately 16.8%. Another $355.2 million of unrecognized stock compensation was scheduled to be recognized over 2.9 years.

GAAP gross margin was 32% and non-GAAP gross margin was 50%. These are weak economics for an enterprise-software company. GAAP operating margin remains deeply negative, so ROIC and the ROIC-WACC spread are decisively negative. PEG, P/E, and EV/FCF are not meaningful while earnings and normalized free cash flow remain negative.

## Commercial proof

The strongest assets are C3.ai's enterprise AI platform, regulated-industry and government footprint, Microsoft alliance, and large cash position. Q1 agreements included Ford, Johnson & Johnson, Heidelberg Materials, Holcim, the Defense Logistics Agency, and the U.S. Marine Corps.

The critical weakness is new-customer monetization. The 10-Q says only 1% of Q1 subscription revenue came from new customers versus 32% a year earlier. One customer entity generated 15% of quarterly revenue. Remaining performance obligations were $200 million, with $133 million expected over the next twelve months, but $50 million consisted of non-cancellable commitments without specified products or quantities.

## Decision framework

### Bull case

Bookings convert to revenue, subscription growth turns positive, GAAP gross margin recovers above 50%, quarterly revenue moves above $60 million, operating losses narrow without increasing SBC, and the Microsoft/industrial channel produces repeatable new-logo revenue. Under those conditions, a return toward $16-$20 is plausible.

### Base case

Revenue remains near $51 million-$57 million per quarter, the FY2027 decline lands near the midpoint, losses and dilution persist, and the stock trades roughly between $8 and $13.

### Bear case

Bookings fail to convert, revenue falls below guidance, gross margin stays depressed, and restructuring creates further customer or employee disruption. The stock could revisit $6-$8 despite the cash balance.

## What would change the quality rating

Require at least two consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth, positive year-over-year subscription growth, GAAP gross margin above 50%, SBC below 50% of revenue and falling, and annual share-count growth below 5%. Until then, treat the position as an event-driven turnaround with small sizing rather than a core compounder.
